FROM BLACKBOARD TO RECOVERY

A lecture that survived as a voice without its drawings.

Feynman’s planetary-motion lecture was not lost because nobody preserved a recording. It was lost because the proof lived in the conversation between a voice and a blackboard—and the blackboard sequence disappeared from the record.

  1. 1964 / CALTECH

    An extra lecture for freshmen.

    In March 1964, Feynman returned to the Caltech freshman class for a special lecture on planetary motion. He set himself a severe constraint: recover Newton’s result using elementary geometry rather than the sophisticated conic machinery Newton had used.

  2. 1965 / OMITTED

    The lecture falls outside the canonical set.

    Robert Leighton received Feynman’s handwritten notes and sketches, but this special lecture was not included in the final 1965 volume of The Feynman Lectures on Physics, which centered on quantum mechanics. An audiotape and a few pages of notes remained.

  3. 1992–94 / RECOVERED

    Enough traces to reconstruct an argument.

    While clearing Leighton’s office files, archivist Judith Goodstein found the unfinished material. David and Judith Goodstein later worked from the tape, transcript, Feynman’s sparse sketches, and Newton’s diagrams to recover the missing geometric sequence.

WHAT THIS INSTRUMENT RE-OPENS

Two spaces, one shared instant.

On the horizontal plane, equal increments of mean anomaly make equal-time wedges from the Sun. On the vertical plane, the corresponding velocity changes close into a circle. The dashed bridge is not a physical connection: it marks the same instant in those two mathematical spaces.

That is the wager of the lecture. If the velocity construction is circular and displaced, the position orbit has the geometry of a conic—with the Sun at a focus.

Historical account adapted from David and Judith Goodstein’s Caltech account of Feynman’s Lost Lecture. The hodograph circle predates Feynman and is associated with Hamilton (1846). This instrument is a contemporary spatial interpretation, not a reproduction of the original classroom performance.
